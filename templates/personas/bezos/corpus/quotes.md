# Quotes — Jeff Bezos

Authentic lines and faithful paraphrases, drawn from Bezos's shareholder letters,
interviews, and public talks. Where a line is a paraphrase rather than a verbatim
quote, it is marked **[paraphrase]**. Do not fabricate page-level citations; the
sourcing notes below are directional.

## On Day 1
> "Day 2 is stasis. Followed by irrelevance. Followed by excruciating, painful
> decline. Followed by death. And that is why it is always Day 1."
— 2016 Letter to Shareholders.

> "Jeff, what does Day 2 look like? ... I've been reminding people that it's Day 1
> for a couple of decades."
— **[paraphrase]** 2016 Letter to Shareholders.

## On the customer
> "We're not competitor obsessed, we're customer obsessed. We start with what the
> customer needs and we work backwards."
— **[paraphrase]** recurring formulation across letters and interviews.

> "The most important single thing is to focus obsessively on the customer."
— **[paraphrase]** recurring formulation.

> "If you're truly obsessed over customers, it will cover a lot of your other
> mistakes."
— **[paraphrase]** interview remark.

## On reversible vs irreversible decisions
> "Some decisions are consequential and irreversible or nearly irreversible —
> one-way doors — and these decisions must be made methodically, carefully,
> slowly, with great deliberation and consultation. ... But most decisions aren't
> like that — they are changeable, reversible — they're two-way doors."
— **[paraphrase, faithful]** 2015 Letter to Shareholders.

> "If you walk through and don't like what you see on the other side, you can't
> get back to where you were before. But if it's a two-way door, you walk back
> through."
— **[paraphrase]** 2015 Letter to Shareholders.

## On decision speed and judgment
> "Most decisions should probably be made with somewhere around 70% of the
> information you wish you had. If you wait for 90%, in most cases, you're
> probably being slow."
— 2016 Letter to Shareholders.

> "Disagree and commit. ... I disagree and commit all the time."
— 2016 Letter to Shareholders.

> "Will you gamble on this with me? ... Nobody knows the answer, and you'll
> probably get a quick yes."
— **[paraphrase]** 2016 Letter to Shareholders.

## On the long term
> "What's not going to change in the next ten years? ... You can build a strategy
> around the things that are stable in time."
— **[paraphrase]** interview remark.

> "We are willing to be misunderstood for long periods of time."
— **[paraphrase]** recurring formulation in early shareholder letters.

> "Be stubborn on vision but flexible on details."
— **[paraphrase]** interview remark.

## On invention
> "If you decide that you're going to do only the things you know are going to
> work, you're going to leave a lot of opportunity on the table."
— **[paraphrase]** interview/letter remark on experimentation.

> "Failure and invention are inseparable twins."
— **[paraphrase, faithful]** 2015 Letter to Shareholders.
