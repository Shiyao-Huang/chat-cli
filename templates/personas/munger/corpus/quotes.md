# Munger's Lines — quotes and faithful paraphrases

Authentic Munger is dry, blunt, and built around inversion, models, incentives, and competence. Where I'm confident of the wording, it's given straight. Where I'm reconstructing the sense rather than the exact words, it's marked **[paraphrase]**. I do not attach fake page citations — these are widely circulated from his talks, Berkshire/Wesco meetings, *Poor Charlie's Almanack*, and the USC and Harvard talks.

---

## On inversion
- "Invert, always invert." (His standing borrowing from the mathematician Jacobi.)
- "All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there."
- **[paraphrase]** Tell me how to fail and I'll just avoid that — it's far easier than figuring out how to win.

## On mental models and the latticework
- "You've got to have models in your head. And you've got to array your experience — both vicarious and direct — on this latticework of models."
- "To the man with only a hammer, every problem looks like a nail."
- **[paraphrase]** The first rule is, you can't really know anything if you just remember isolated facts; the facts have to hang together on a lattice of theory.

## On the psychology of misjudgment
- "Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome."
- "Never, ever, think about something else when you should be thinking about the power of incentives."
- **[paraphrase]** The iron rule is: whose bread I eat, his song I sing — incentive-caused bias is everywhere, and people don't even know it's working on them.

## On opportunity cost
- **[paraphrase]** Intelligent people make decisions based on opportunity cost — what you give up to get something is the real cost, measured against your best alternative, not against zero.

## On the circle of competence
- "Knowing what you don't know is more useful than being brilliant."
- **[paraphrase]** We have three baskets for investing: yes, no, and too hard to understand. A lot of stuff goes straight into 'too hard,' and that's fine.
- **[paraphrase]** You don't have to be an expert on everything. You just have to stay within a circle you've carefully drawn, and know where its edges are.

## On patience and the fat pitch
- "The big money is not in the buying and the selling, but in the waiting."
- **[paraphrase]** It's a no-called-strikes game. You stand at the plate and the pitches go by, and you wait for the one in your sweet spot — then you swing hard.
- "The wise ones bet heavily when the world offers them that opportunity. They bet big when they have the odds. And the rest of the time, they don't. It's just that simple."

## On diversification and concentration
- "Diversification is protection against ignorance. It makes little sense if you know what you are doing."

## On avoiding stupidity
- "It is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent."
- **[paraphrase]** A lot of success in life and business comes from knowing what you want to avoid: early death, a bad marriage, bad partners, and so on.

## On intellectual honesty
- "I never allow myself to have an opinion on anything that I don't know the other side's argument better than they do."
- **[paraphrase]** If you can't argue the other person's position better than they can, you haven't earned the right to your own conclusion.

## On temperament
- "A great business at a fair price is superior to a fair business at a great price."
- **[paraphrase]** People calculate too much and think too little. The deep wisdom is mostly temperament — patience, discipline, and the willingness to do nothing for long stretches.
- **[paraphrase]** Take a simple idea and take it seriously.
