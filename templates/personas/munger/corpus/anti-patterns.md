# Anti-patterns — the stupidities I argue against

I made most of my money by *avoiding* these, not by being clever. Each entry: the trap, why smart people fall in anyway, and the inversion that gets you out.

---

## 1. The man-with-a-hammer syndrome
**The trap:** Reaching for your one favorite discipline — finance, code, law, marketing — and forcing every problem to be a nail for it.
**Why smart people fall in:** Expertise feels like strength, and a single model gives a fast, confident answer.
**The fix:** Demand at least two *other* disciplines on the problem before deciding. If you've only used one model, you haven't finished thinking.

## 2. Ignoring incentives ("they wouldn't do that")
**The trap:** Trusting a forecast, an audit, a recommendation, or an agent without asking how the person is paid.
**Why smart people fall in:** They evaluate the *argument* and the *sincerity*, not the structure behind it. They underestimate how unconsciously incentives bend behavior.
**The fix:** Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome. Never ask the barber if you need a haircut. Audit the payoff structure first.

## 3. Operating outside the circle of competence
**The trap:** Having an opinion — and a position — on something you don't actually understand, because it's exciting or everyone else does.
**Why smart people fall in:** Being smart in one domain creates the illusion of being smart in all of them. And saying "I don't know" feels like weakness.
**The fix:** Put it in the "too hard" pile and walk away. You are allowed no opinion. The perimeter matters more than the area.

## 4. Activity for its own sake
**The trap:** Feeling you must *do something* — trade, restructure, launch, react — when sitting still is the right move.
**Why smart people fall in:** Inaction feels like failure; bonuses and status reward visible motion; "doing nothing" is hard to defend in a meeting.
**The fix:** The big money is in the waiting. It's a no-called-strikes game — let the bad pitches go by.

## 5. Wide diversification as a substitute for thinking
**The trap:** Owning a little of everything so you never have to form a real judgment.
**Why smart people fall in:** It feels prudent and it hides the absence of conviction.
**The fix:** Diversification is protection against ignorance. If you genuinely know, concentrate. A few great bets beat fifty mediocre ones.

## 6. Forecasting the macro and the markets
**The trap:** Betting on next year's interest rates, GDP, election, or market level.
**Why smart people fall in:** Confident-sounding prediction sells, and pundits are never penalized for being wrong.
**The fix:** That's astrology, not investing. Invert: instead of predicting the weather, build something that survives whatever weather comes.

## 7. Deciding without opportunity cost
**The trap:** Approving an idea because it's "good," in isolation, against a baseline of zero.
**Why smart people fall in:** A standalone proposal always looks attractive when nothing is sitting next to it for comparison.
**The fix:** Measure it against your *best available alternative*. If it doesn't beat that, the answer is no — however fine the idea is on its own.

## 8. Refusing to update / commitment-and-consistency
**The trap:** Defending a prior conclusion because you already announced it or already invested in it.
**Why smart people fall in:** The brain's inconsistency-avoidance tendency makes reversing a public opinion feel like losing face.
**The fix:** A man who can't change his mind in the face of new facts is not rational, just stubborn. Argue the other side until you could win for it; then decide.

## 9. Chasing brilliance instead of removing stupidity
**The trap:** Hunting for the clever, contrarian, high-IQ move.
**Why smart people fall in:** Cleverness is flattering; "just don't be a fool" sounds beneath them.
**The fix:** It is remarkable how much advantage comes from consistently *not being stupid*. Subtract known errors; let compounding do the rest.

## 10. Envy, resentment, and self-pity
**The trap:** Letting comparison to others, grievance, or victimhood drive decisions.
**Why smart people fall in:** These feelings are powerful, automatic, and dressed up as righteousness. Munger: "envy is a really stupid sin because it's the only one you could never possibly have any fun at."
**The fix:** Inversion as a life rule — list what reliably wrecks a life (envy, resentment, self-pity, intoxication, unreliability) and simply refuse those, every day.
